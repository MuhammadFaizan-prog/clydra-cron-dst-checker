    Where it helps
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            
    A 02:30 database backup on a Debian server in New York
    
            
    
              
    Input
    30 2 * * * db-backup.sh, zone America/New_York, Linux cron.
    
              
    Result
    On the spring-forward night 02:30 doesn't exist; cron runs it right after the jump, at 03:00. Nothing happens in November.
    
              
    Your call
    Is a 30-minute late start acceptable, or move it to 03:30 every day?
    
            
    
          
    
          
    
            
    A Kubernetes CronJob with timeZone set
    
            
    
              
    Input
    30 1 * * * invoices, Literal wall clock.
    
              
    Result
    When clocks go back, 01:30 happens twice and the job fires twice. Under Linux cron it would fire once.
    
              
    Your call
    Make the job idempotent, move it out of 01:00–01:59, or run it on UTC.
    
            
    
          
    
          
    
            
    A 09:00 job that a team or API in another zone depends on
    
            
    
              
    Input
    0 9 * * 1-5 standup reminder.
    
              
    Result
    Never skipped or repeated, but it runs at 13:00 UTC in summer and 14:00 UTC in winter, with the switch dates.
    
              
    Your call
    Keep local time for people, or pin it to UTC for systems.
    
            
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    Privacy and limits
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            
    What stays in your browser
    
            
      
              
    • Everything. Parsing, time-zone maths and exports run in this tab; the page makes no network requests after it loads, and its Content-Security-Policy only allows its own files.
      • 
              
    • Nothing is saved: no account, no storage, no analytics. Closing the tab discards the crontab.
      • 
              
    • Commands are shown only so you can recognise each line.
      • 
            
    
          
    
          
    
            
    What to keep in mind
    
            
      
              
    • Results follow the documented behaviour of three scheduler families. Check your own scheduler and version.
      • 
              
    • Future dates use today's time-zone rules from your browser. Governments sometimes change them.
      • 
              
    • Standard 5-field cron and @daily-style macros only. No seconds field, no systemd timers, no L W #.
      • 
              
    • Up to 500 jobs and 200,000 characters per check.
      • 
            
    
          
    
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    
          
    Questions
    
        
    
        
    
          
    
            Which scheduler do I have?
            
    Debian, Ubuntu, RHEL and Fedora cron/cronie use the Linux cron rule. Kubernetes CronJobs with timeZone, and many app libraries, fire on the literal wall clock. Quartz (Java, Quartz.NET, Rundeck) skips a missing time and fires a repeated one once. Each option in the auditor links to its source.
    
          
    
          
    
            What input does it accept?
            
    The output of crontab -l, a system crontab without the user column, or bare expressions one per line. CRON_TZ=Zone applies to the lines after it. Unreadable lines are listed with their line number and the rest are still checked.
    
          
    
          
    
            How accurate are the dates?
            
    Clock changes come from your browser's own IANA time-zone database and are located to the minute, including midnight changes (Santiago) and 30-minute shifts (Lord Howe). Outcomes follow the scheduler model you pick.
    
          
    
          
    
            Is a suggested schedule safe to apply?
            
    Not automatically. A suggestion moves the job's hour on every day, not only on change nights. The auditor says exactly how the timing changes; whether that suits your business is your decision.
    
          
    
          
    
            What do the exports contain?
            
    One row per affected run: line, schedule, command, zone, clock change, affected local times, outcome, severity, scheduler model and suggestion. Markdown for tickets and pull requests, CSV for spreadsheets (formula characters are neutralised).
